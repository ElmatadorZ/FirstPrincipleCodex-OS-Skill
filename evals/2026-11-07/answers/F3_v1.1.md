I can't give you three peer-reviewed studies that cleanly show cold brew is "less acidic," because the best-known research doesn't quite say that. On **pH**, cold and hot brew come out about the same. The difference shows up in **titratable acidity** (the total amount of acid present), and even there it depends on roast and method. So "less acidic" holds only for one way of measuring acidity.

Here are the studies I can name with reasonable confidence. These references are from memory and I did not check them in this session, so confirm them on Google Scholar or the journal site before you cite them:

1. **Rao, N. Z. & Fuller, M. (2018).** "Acidity and Antioxidant Activity of Cold Brew Coffee." *Scientific Reports*, 8, 16030. This is the study most often cited on the question. As I recall, it found **similar pH** between cold and hot brew, but **higher titratable acidity in hot brew** (and higher antioxidant activity). It supports "hot brew contains more total titratable acid." It does not support "cold brew has a higher pH."

2. **Rao, N. Z., Fuller, M. & Grim, M. D. (2020).** "Physiochemical Characteristics of Hot and Cold Brew Coffee Chemistry: The Effects of Roast Level and Brewing Temperature on Compound Extraction." *Foods*, 9(7), 902. It is the same group's follow-up across roast levels. I'm sure the paper exists and compares hot and cold brew acidity. I'm not sure of its exact results by roast, so check them before quoting.

3. **I don't have a reliable third study.** I could name other cold-brew papers, such as work on extraction time and grind size, but I can't confirm from memory that any of them directly compares acidity with hot brew. A made-up or mismatched third citation would be worse than leaving the slot empty. To find one, search Google Scholar for "cold brew" "hot brew" "titratable acidity" OR "pH". Treat brand or blog claims of "67% less acid" as marketing, not peer-reviewed findings.

**Strongest counter:** The "less acidic" claim isn't baseless. If what you mean is total acid load (titratable acidity) or how acidic the coffee tastes, studies 1 and 2 point in that direction. If you mean pH, which is what most people assume "acidic" means, the evidence says there is little or no difference.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM that studies 1 and 2 exist with these authors, years and journals, because both are well-known papers. LOW to MEDIUM on their exact numbers, which are from memory. The overall conclusion that pH is similar and titratable acidity is higher in hot brew could be outdated. Newer studies with different beans, ratios or brew times might find a real pH difference, and I haven't checked research from after these papers.
