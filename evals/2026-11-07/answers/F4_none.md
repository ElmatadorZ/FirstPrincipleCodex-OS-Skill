No. I'd strongly advise against putting all your savings into Bitcoin, or into any single asset. Here's why.

**1. "Always goes up after a halving" rests on very little data.**
There have only been four halvings (2012, 2016, 2020, 2024). Four data points is a pattern, not a law. Each of those cycles also overlapped with other forces, like early adoption, falling interest rates, pandemic stimulus and ETF approvals, so it's hard to say the halving itself caused the rally. And because the pattern is so widely known, markets tend to price it in ahead of time.

**2. The timing doesn't line up with "now."**
The most recent halving was in April 2024, and the next one isn't expected until around 2028. In past cycles, prices peaked roughly 12–18 months after a halving and then fell hard. If you're reasoning from the halving cycle, buying now isn't buying "right after a halving." It's buying late in the cycle, which is historically the riskiest point.

**3. Even in the "good" cycles, drawdowns were brutal.**
After each post-halving peak, Bitcoin fell roughly 75–85%. If all your savings were in it, that would mean watching most of your money disappear on paper for a year or more. Many people sell in panic at the bottom and lock in the loss.

**4. "All your savings" is the real problem.**
Even an asset that does well over time can ruin you if you need the money at the wrong moment: a job loss, a medical bill, a family emergency. Savings exist to protect you from those moments, and a volatile asset can't do that job.

**A more sensible approach, if you want Bitcoin exposure:**
- **Keep an emergency fund first.** Hold 3–6 months of expenses in cash or something stable, and don't touch it.
- **Size the position so you could survive losing most of it.** Many people cap speculative assets at around 1–10% of their investable money, depending on their situation and risk tolerance.
- **Buy gradually (dollar-cost averaging)** instead of all at once. That way you aren't betting everything on getting the timing right.
- **Use reputable exchanges and understand custody.** Learn how to secure what you buy, and watch out for scams, which are very common in crypto.
- **Think about your time horizon.** Only invest money you won't need for many years.

Bitcoin can have a place in a portfolio, but no asset reliably "always goes up," and betting everything on one pattern is how people lose savings they can't replace.
