He didn't. That's a popular myth.

Einstein was very good at math from an early age. By about 15 he had taught himself calculus, and he did very well in mathematics and physics throughout school. When someone showed him a 1935 "Ripley's Believe It or Not!" item claiming he had failed math, he said: "I never failed in mathematics. Before I was fifteen I had mastered differential and integral calculus."

The myth probably comes from a few real events that got garbled:

- **He failed an entrance exam.** In 1895, at 16, about two years younger than usual, he took the entrance exam for the Swiss Federal Polytechnic in Zurich. His math and physics scores were excellent, but he fell short in non-science subjects such as French, chemistry and biology. He went to a school in Aarau, Switzerland, for a year and was admitted the next year.
- **The grading scale flipped.** The Aarau school graded from 1 to 6, with 6 the best. At one point the scale had run the other way, with 1 the best. Someone looking at his record later could easily read his top marks of 6 as failing grades.
- **He didn't get along with rigid schooling.** He disliked rote learning and authoritarian teachers, especially at his Munich gymnasium, which he left without a diploma. A few teachers saw him as a difficult student, and over time that turned into "bad student."

The "failed math" story stuck because people like the idea that a genius was once a struggling student. In reality, he struggled with the school system, not with math.
