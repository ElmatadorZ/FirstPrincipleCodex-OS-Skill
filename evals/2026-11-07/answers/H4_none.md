The idea that we only use 10% of our brains is a myth. There's no hidden 90% waiting to be unlocked. You already use all of your brain.

**Why it isn't true**

- **Brain imaging.** fMRI and PET scans show activity across almost the whole brain over a normal day. Different regions work harder for different tasks, but no large area sits unused.
- **Brain damage.** If 90% of the brain did nothing, damage to most areas would cause no symptoms. In reality, even a small stroke in almost any region causes noticeable problems with movement, speech, memory, vision or personality.
- **Energy cost.** The brain is about 2% of your body weight but uses roughly 20% of your energy. Evolution would not keep such a costly organ if most of it were idle.
- **Unused neurons die off.** Neurons that aren't used tend to be pruned away. A large dormant region would waste away rather than wait to be switched on.

The myth probably came from misquoted early psychology, such as William James saying people use only a fraction of their *potential*, plus self-help marketing and films like *Lucy* and *Limitless*.

**What you can actually improve**

You can't switch on unused brain regions, but you can make your brain work better. These have real evidence behind them:

1. **Sleep.** Getting 7 to 9 hours helps you lock in memories and clears out metabolic waste. Few things damage focus and learning as much as poor sleep.
2. **Exercise.** Regular aerobic activity is one of the best-supported ways to improve memory and executive function. It boosts BDNF, a protein that supports neuron growth, and helps the hippocampus.
3. **Smart learning methods:**
   - *Retrieval practice:* test yourself instead of rereading.
   - *Spaced repetition:* review material at growing intervals (Anki is a popular app for this).
   - *Interleaving:* mix different types of problems instead of practicing one type in a block.
4. **Deliberate practice.** Work just beyond your current skill level and get fast feedback. This is how expertise and lasting brain changes actually develop.
5. **Single-tasking.** Switching between tasks has a real cost. Working on one thing at a time, in protected blocks, usually gets more done.
6. **Stress management.** Chronic stress hurts memory and decision-making. Exercise, sleep, social connection and practices like mindfulness can help.
7. **General health.** A balanced diet (for example, Mediterranean-style), not smoking, limiting alcohol and keeping blood pressure under control all protect your brain over the long term.
8. **Staying socially and mentally active.** Learning new, challenging things such as a language, an instrument or a complex skill is linked to better cognitive health as you age.

Be skeptical of "brain-unlocking" pills, nootropic stacks, and brain-training games that promise big general gains. The evidence mostly shows you get better at the specific game, with little effect on general intelligence.

**Bottom line:** you don't have a locked 90% to open. You get the most out of your brain through sleep, exercise, good learning habits and focused practice.
