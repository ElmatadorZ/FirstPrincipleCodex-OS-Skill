**Short answer: probably not, at least not as a rewrite.** With 6 engineers and 50k users, a full microservices rewrite will most likely cost you more than it returns. Unless you have a specific bottleneck that only a service split can fix, extract one piece at a time, and only where you've measured a need.

**Ariya4**
- **Problem:** not yet stated. "Should we do microservices" is a solution looking for a problem.
- **Cause:** likely candidates are coupling inside the codebase, deploy friction, or a specific performance hotspot.
- **Cessation:** the team ships independently and fast, and the system handles 5–10x current load.
- **Path:** a modular monolith, then targeted extraction only where it pays off.

📍 **SYSTEM MAP**
```
Services count → Ops overhead (CI/CD, tracing, network failures, data consistency)
     → Engineer time on infra ↑ → Feature velocity ↓ → Pressure to "fix architecture" → more services
     ↑____________________________________________________________________|  (R: reinforcing trap)
```
- **Leverage:** enforcing module boundaries *inside* Django (separate apps, explicit interfaces, no cross-app model imports, `import-linter`). You get about 80% of the decoupling at about 10% of the cost.
- **Dead weight:** a service mesh, distributed transactions, and per-service CI pipelines kept up by 6 people. With 5+ services that's under one engineer per service, plus on-call.
- **Horizon:** medium to long. A rewrite typically freezes features for 6–18 months.

📍 **COMPOUND MIND: all the paths to "faster team + scalable system"**
1. **Fix the monolith in place:** profiling, query optimisation, caching, read replicas, Celery for heavy work. *Cheapest, fastest payoff.*
2. **Modular monolith:** split into well-bounded Django apps with explicit internal APIs. *Best structural payoff for your size.*
3. **Selective extraction (strangler fig):** pull out 1–2 components with clearly different needs, such as a CPU-heavy job, a component with a different scaling profile, or one with different compliance requirements. *Do this only when you can measure the reason.*
4. **Full rewrite to microservices:** *highest cost and risk, slowest payoff.*

**Optimal path:** 1 → 2 → (3 only if data justifies it). Skip 4.
**Cross-domain parallel:** Conway's Law. Your architecture tends to mirror your org chart. With one team, one deployable unit is the natural fit. Microservices start to pay off once you have several teams that keep blocking each other.

📍 **SYNTHESIS**
Don't rewrite. Do this instead:
1. **Name the pain.** Write down the 2–3 concrete problems, such as "deploys take 40 min" or "checkout p95 is 3s".
2. **Measure.** Use APM or profiling (Sentry, New Relic, Django Silk), slow-query logs, and CI timings.
3. **Fix in place:** DB indexes, caching, Celery, faster test suite, CI parallelisation.
4. **Modularise:** set clear app boundaries and enforce import rules. This makes a future split cheap if you ever need one.
5. **Extract only with evidence.** If one component clearly needs independent scaling or deploys, strangle it out as a single service. Learn what it costs to operate before you add a second.

**Revisit microservices when:** you have about 3+ teams stepping on each other, or a measured bottleneck that can't be solved inside the monolith.
