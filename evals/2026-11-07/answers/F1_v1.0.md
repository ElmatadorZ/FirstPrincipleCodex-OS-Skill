**Short answer: he didn't.** The question rests on a myth. Einstein was strong at mathematics from childhood, and the records we have show top marks in it.

📍 **REALITY ANCHOR**
- **Known (well documented):**
  - Einstein taught himself algebra and Euclidean geometry around age 12. By his own account, he had mastered differential and integral calculus before he was 15.
  - His 1896 school-leaving certificate (Matura) from the cantonal school in Aarau, Switzerland, shows 6s in algebra, geometry and physics. A 6 was the highest grade on that scale.
  - In 1895, at 16, he took the entrance exam for the Zurich Polytechnic (ETH) about two years early. He failed the overall exam because of weak non-science sections such as French and other general subjects. His math and physics scores were excellent.
  - When a 1935 *Ripley's Believe It or Not!* item claimed he had failed math, Einstein denied it. He said he had never failed in mathematics.
- **Inferred (likely, but not fully proven):** The myth probably grew from several things at once. People misremembered the failed ETH entrance exam as "failing math." The Swiss 1–6 grading scale is easy to misread by anyone used to a system where 1 is best. And the "genius who flunked school" story is simply appealing.
- **Unknown:** Exactly which of these sources started the myth. Nobody can trace it with certainty.

📍 **AXIOM GATE**
- Assumption 1: "Einstein failed math." **This is false.** What remains true is that he failed one entrance exam, and not because of math.
- Assumption 2: "School grades predict genius." This is weak at best. It is the hidden premise that keeps the myth alive.
- Assumption 3: "A widely repeated story must be true." This fails Kalama tests 1 (rumor) and 9 (consensus without an evidence chain). The primary documents contradict it.
- Ariya4: **Problem:** a false belief. **Cause:** a misremembered exam plus misread grades plus an appealing story. **Cessation:** the belief is replaced by the documented record. **Path:** go back to the primary sources: his grades and his own denial.

📍 **SHADOW GATE**
- **Mirror:** The question takes the myth as fact and asks *why*. That framing invites a made-up explanation.
- **Inversion:** "He really did fail math." Strength: **LOW.** No primary record supports it, and the surviving records contradict it.
- **Blind Spot:** A genuine school record showing a failing math grade would overturn this answer. None is known to exist.
- **Interest Map:** Motivational speakers, ads and "late bloomer" stories gain from the myth, because it makes a comforting point. Ripley's gained from surprising claims.
- **Meta-Void:** **OBVIOUS.** The answer is clear, so more analysis adds nothing.
- **Verdict:** **CONSISTENT.**

📍 **SYNTHESIS**
Einstein did not fail math in school. He was a gifted mathematics student who taught himself calculus as a teenager. He did fail one university entrance exam in 1895, when he was two years younger than usual. That failure came from the language and general-knowledge sections, and his math scores were excellent. He passed the next year after finishing school in Aarau with top marks in math and physics. The "Einstein failed math" story is a myth that he denied himself.

📍 **CONFIDENCE FIELD**
- **CONFIDENCE:** 95% that he did not fail math. About 70% on the exact reasons the myth spread.
- **SHADOW VERDICT:** CONSISTENT
- **UNKNOWNS:** Who started the myth, and how much each suspected cause (grade-scale confusion, the ETH exam, Ripley's) contributed to it.
- **FAILURE COND:** This answer would be wrong if an authentic school record turned up showing a failing math grade.
