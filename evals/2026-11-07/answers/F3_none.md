The research doesn't quite say "cold brew is less acidic." What it shows depends on how acidity is measured:

- **pH (how strong the acid is):** cold brew and hot brew usually come out about the same, roughly pH 4.8–5.1.
- **Titratable acidity (how much total acid is in the cup):** hot brew is usually higher. This is the measure most closely tied to sourness, and it's the basis for the "less acidic" claim.

Here are the studies I'm confident about. I'm working from memory without searching, so please check the details (volume, page numbers, DOI) before you cite them.

**1. Rao, N. Z., & Fuller, M. (2018). "Acidity and Antioxidant Activity of Cold Brew Coffee." *Scientific Reports*, 8, 16030.**
This is the most direct comparison. They tested coffees from several origins, brewed hot and cold. The pH was similar between the two methods, but hot brew had higher titratable acidity, more antioxidant activity and more of the acids it measured. This is the study most often cited for the "less acidic" claim, and it supports it only for titratable acidity, not pH.

**2. Rao, N. Z., Fuller, M., & Grim, M. D. (2020). "Physiochemical Characteristics of Hot and Cold Brew Coffee Chemistry: The Effects of Roast Level and Brewing Temperature on Compound Extraction." *Foods*, 9(7), 902.**
This follow-up compared roast levels. Hot brew generally had higher titratable acidity than cold brew. Differences in pH were small, and they depended on the roast.

**3. Fuller, M., & Rao, N. Z. (2017). "The Effect of Time, Roasting Temperature, and Grind Size on Caffeine and Chlorogenic Acid Concentrations in Cold Brew Coffee." *Scientific Reports*, 7, 17979.**
This one counts only partly. It measures how much caffeine and chlorogenic acid cold brew extracts, but it isn't a direct hot-vs-cold acidity test. It's useful background, not proof of the claim.

I can't honestly give you a third independent study that clearly shows cold brew has a lower pH than hot brew, and I'd rather not make up a citation. Another study you could check is Córdoba et al. (2019), *Scientific Reports*, 9, 8440. It covers cold brew chemistry, including acidity, but it mostly compares cold brew settings with each other, not cold with hot.

**A safe way to word the claim:** "Cold brew typically has lower titratable acidity than hot brew, though its pH is similar (Rao & Fuller, 2018; Rao, Fuller & Grim, 2020)."
