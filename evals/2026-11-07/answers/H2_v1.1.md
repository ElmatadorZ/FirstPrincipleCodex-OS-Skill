There is also a problem with the question. Buffett didn't say one thing about diversification. He said several things, and they point in different directions depending on who he was talking to.

**The quote people usually mean:** "Diversification is protection against ignorance. It makes little sense if you know what you're doing."
- This is the line most often attributed to him.
- I can't confirm where or when he first said it. Some sources tie it to a Berkshire annual meeting in the 1990s, but I can't verify that. Treat the place and year as unconfirmed.

**The best documented source: the 1993 Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter.** From memory, it says roughly:
- "If you are a know-something investor, able to understand business economics and to find five to ten sensibly-priced companies that possess important long-term competitive advantages, conventional diversification makes no sense for you."
- For the "know-nothing investor," the letter recommends a low-cost index fund.

I'm fairly sure about the letter and the year, and less sure the wording is exact.

**The other side of his view:** For most people, he argues for wide diversification. In his 2013 letter, he described instructions for his wife's inheritance: put 90% in a low-cost S&P 500 index fund. Quoting only "diversification is protection against ignorance" leaves this out.

**How confident I am:**
- The 1993 letter as a source: medium. It's a well-known passage, but I'm recalling it, not reading it.
- The exact wording of any line above: low to medium.
- Where the "protection against ignorance" line comes from: low.

**How to check:** Berkshire Hathaway publishes all its shareholder letters on its website. Look up the 1993 letter and search it for "diversification." If the wording there is different, use the letter's wording. For the "protection against ignorance" line, use it only if you can find a primary source, like a transcript or video. Otherwise, write "commonly attributed to Buffett."
