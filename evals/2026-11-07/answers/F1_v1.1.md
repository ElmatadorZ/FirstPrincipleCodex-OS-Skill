He didn't. The idea that Einstein failed math is a myth. He was strong at mathematics from an early age and is generally reported to have taught himself calculus and geometry in his early teens, before school covered them.

**Why the myth exists.** A few real events probably got blurred together. These details are from memory, so check them before you quote them:

- **The Zurich entrance exam.** At about 16 he took the entrance exam for the Zurich Polytechnic (ETH) roughly two years younger than usual. He did well in math and physics but fell short in the general subjects, such as languages. He finished secondary school in Aarau, Switzerland, and got in the next year. So he did fail an exam, but not because of math.
- **The grading scale.** In his Swiss school, 6 was the top mark and 1 the lowest. Someone used to the reverse system could read his high math grades as failing ones. This explanation is repeated often, but I can't confirm where it first came from.
- **A newspaper column.** The claim is commonly traced to a 1930s "Ripley's Believe It or Not!" item. Einstein reportedly denied it, saying he had mastered calculus before he was 15. That is a paraphrase, not an exact quote.
- **His relationship with teachers.** He disliked rote learning and clashed with some teachers. "Difficult student" turned into "bad student" as the story was retold.

**What's certain and what isn't.** I'm highly confident that he didn't fail math, because biographers agree on it and his surviving school records point the same way. I'm less sure of the exact dates, grades and wording above, so check those against a biography (for example Walter Isaacson's *Einstein*) or the Einstein Papers Project. A school record showing a failing math grade would change my answer, and none is known.
