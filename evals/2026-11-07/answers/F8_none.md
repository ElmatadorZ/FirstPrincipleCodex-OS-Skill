Short answer: no, not on average. When the total calorie cut is the same, intermittent fasting (IF) and ordinary daily calorie restriction produce about the same weight loss. IF is another way to eat less. It doesn't seem to have a special fat-burning effect.

**What the research shows**

- **Head-to-head trials:** Randomized trials have compared alternate-day fasting, 5:2, and time-restricted eating (such as eating only within an 8-hour window) with standard calorie restriction. Most find similar weight loss, usually around 3–8% of body weight over 6–12 months. Meta-analyses agree, and any differences they find tend to be small and not clinically meaningful.
- **Time-restricted eating with calories matched:** In a well-known 2022 trial in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (Liu et al.), both groups were held to the same calorie target. Adding an 8-hour eating window gave no significant extra weight loss over a year.
- **Metabolic markers:** Some studies report small improvements in insulin sensitivity or blood pressure, especially when eating is done earlier in the day. Overall these benefits look modest, and most of them come from the weight loss itself.
- **Muscle loss:** Some trials suggest IF may lose a bit more lean mass. You can partly offset this with enough protein and resistance training.
- **Dropout:** Dropout rates are similar between the two approaches. Alternate-day fasting sometimes has *higher* dropout, because fasting days are hard for some people.

**Why IF works for some people anyway**

IF can make it easier to eat less, even if it isn't better per calorie. It sets a simple rule ("no food after 7 pm"), cuts out late-night snacking, and means you don't have to count calories. If that suits how you live, IF may help *you* more than calorie counting would. For other people, fasting leads to strong hunger, overeating later, or irritability, and steady daily restriction works better.

**Who should be careful**

Get medical advice before trying IF if you:
- have diabetes, especially if you take insulin or sulfonylureas, because of the risk of low blood sugar
- are pregnant or breastfeeding
- have a history of eating disorders
- take medications that need to be taken with food

**Bottom line:** The best diet is the one you can stick to. Both approaches work about equally well. Choose the one that fits your schedule and appetite, eat enough protein, and stay active.
