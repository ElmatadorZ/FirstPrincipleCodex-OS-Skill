Buffett has said several things about diversification, and the best-known line is also the hardest to source. Here is what I can confirm and what I can't. I haven't checked these against the original documents in this session, so treat the exact wording as close but not guaranteed.

**1. The famous one-liner, with a weak source**

> "Diversification is protection against ignorance. It makes little sense if you know what you are doing."

This is the version quoted most often. It's usually credited to Buffett speaking at a Berkshire Hathaway annual meeting in the mid-1990s, with 1996 given most often. Some sources trace it to Robert Hagstrom's book *The Warren Buffett Way* (1994) instead. I don't know of a primary source, such as a transcript, that settles the exact wording, venue or year. If you need to cite it formally, call it "attributed" or quote the letter below.

**2. The shareholder-letter version, with a strong source**

The citable version comes from Buffett's **1993 Chairman's Letter to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders** (published in early 1994):

> "If you are a know-something investor, able to understand business economics and to find five to ten sensibly-priced companies that possess important long-term competitive advantages, conventional diversification makes no sense for you."

The same letter makes the risk argument:

> "We believe that a policy of portfolio concentration may well decrease risk if it raises, as it should, both the intensity with which an investor thinks about a business and the comfort-level he must feel with its economic characteristics before buying into it."

In that letter he also says the opposite applies to the "know-nothing" investor. For that person, broad diversification through a low-cost index fund is the sensible choice. So his view is not "diversification is bad." It depends on whether you actually understand what you own.

**Summary**

- **Safest exact quote to cite:** the 1993 Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter (above).
- **Most-quoted line:** "Diversification is protection against ignorance..." It's widely attributed to Buffett around the mid-1990s, often to the 1996 annual meeting, but there's no confirmed primary source.

To check the wording, read the 1993 letter on berkshirehathaway.com under "Letters."
